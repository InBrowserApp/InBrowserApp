## How to use the compound interest calculator

Enter your starting balance, the amount you plan to add each month, the annual interest rate, and the number of years. Use the same currency for both amounts. Set the monthly contribution to zero to see how a single deposit grows, or set the starting balance to zero to model saving from scratch.

Choose how often interest compounds and whether monthly contributions arrive at the beginning or end of the month. The chart compares your projected balance with the total you put in. Open the year-by-year breakdown to see the balance, contributions, and interest at the end of each year.

## How compound interest works

Compound interest applies to both your principal and the interest you’ve already earned. Without additional deposits, the ending balance is **P × (1 + r/n)^(n × t)**, where **P** is the starting balance, **r** is the annual interest rate as a decimal, **n** is the number of compounding periods per year, and **t** is the time in years.

For monthly contributions, the calculator converts the chosen compounding frequency to an equivalent monthly growth rate. Deposits made at the start of the month get one extra month of growth compared with month-end deposits. The annual interest rate is the nominal rate before compounding. If your account quotes an annual percentage yield (APY), choose annual compounding to use that yield directly.

## Example with monthly contributions

Start with 10,000, add 100 at the end of each month, and enter a 5% annual rate with monthly compounding for 10 years. The estimated balance is **31,998.32**. Of that, **22,000.00** is money you put in and **9,998.32** is interest.

## What the estimate includes

The projection assumes a fixed rate and regular contributions. The calculator leaves out taxes, fees, and inflation. Actual savings or investment returns can vary. Your inputs and calculations stay in your browser.
