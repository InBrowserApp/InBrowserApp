## Transpose a MIDI file and change its tempo

Open a Standard MIDI File (.mid or .midi), set the pitch shift in semitones and choose a tempo percentage. Download the edited MIDI and open it in your sequencer, notation software, or MIDI player. Use the tool to fit a song to a singer’s range, move an instrumental part into a more comfortable register, or make a slower practice version.

## Choose a pitch shift and tempo

Each semitone is one step between adjacent keys on a piano. Enter +2 to move a melody from C to D, or −12 to move it down an octave. Leave the drum option on for General MIDI songs. Channel 10 uses note numbers to select drum sounds, so shifting those numbers can change the kit pattern.

Tempo is a percentage of the original. A song at 120 BPM plays at 90 BPM with 75% or 150 BPM with 125%. If the original song speeds up and slows down, each tempo change is scaled by the same percentage. Leave the pitch shift at 0 when you only want to change the tempo.

## What the edited MIDI keeps

The download keeps the tracks, instrument changes, note velocities, and controller events, along with the original file on your device. The file is processed in your browser. The result is still MIDI, so the instrument sounds depend on the player or virtual instruments you use to open it.
