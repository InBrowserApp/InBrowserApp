## Shorten long pauses in audio

Remove long gaps from a podcast, interview, voice note, or narration without selecting every pause by hand. The tool detects quiet sections in the original waveform, marks the parts that will be removed, and keeps a short pause so the result still has room to breathe.

## How to remove silence from an audio file

1. Open a mono or stereo recording. Common inputs are WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC, and OGG, depending on the codecs your browser supports.
2. Check the marked cuts and the estimated time saved. Adjust the silence threshold or minimum silence length to change which pauses are detected.
3. Choose **Remove long silences**, listen to the edit, and download a 16-bit, 48 kHz WAV. Analysis and export run on your device.

## How should I set the silence threshold?

The threshold in dBFS controls how quiet a passage needs to be. Lower it if the tool marks quiet speech for removal. Raise it a little if steady background sound keeps long pauses from being detected. In stereo files, a passage must be quiet in both channels.

The minimum silence length controls how long a passage has to stay quiet before the tool shortens it. Try a longer minimum for natural conversation or a shorter one for dictation. With the default 0.8-second minimum, a two-second gap is shortened to 0.15 seconds and a half-second pause is left in place.

## What does the tool change?

The tool removes time from quiet passages and applies short fades at the joins. Steady background sound during speech stays in the recording. The waveform and time estimates reflect your current settings, so check the marked regions and listen to the result before you download it.
