## How do I test a gamepad in my browser?

Connect your controller by USB or Bluetooth, keep this tab open, and press any button on the controller. Move each stick and press each button in turn to see which inputs the browser receives. If you have several controllers connected, choose the one you want to test from the gamepad menu.

## How can I tell if a stick is drifting?

Move each stick in a few directions and let it return on its own. An untouched stick should rest near the center, where both X and Y are zero. When stick plots are displayed, select **Check stick drift** and leave both sticks untouched for three seconds. The average offset is the mean distance from center during that check, while the peak offset is the largest distance recorded.

For example, X at 0.030 and Y at 0.040 put a stick 0.050 from center. If repeated checks show about the same offset after you release it, the stick may be drifting. A peak much larger than the average can indicate a brief jump. If the problem shows up in only one game, check its dead zone setting. The dead zone is the range of small movements near center that the game ignores.

## What should I see when testing buttons and triggers?

Press and release each button. A working digital button usually switches between 0% and 100%, while an analog trigger can report values in between. Squeeze each trigger slowly, then release it. Compare whether both triggers reach their full range and return to 0%. For controllers without a standard mapping, move one control at a time to identify its numbered button or axis.

## Why isn't my gamepad detected?

Check that your device recognizes the controller, then return to this tab and press a button again. If Bluetooth is unreliable, try a USB cable that supports data transfer. If the controller works in another app, try opening the tester directly in an up-to-date browser. What the page can read depends on what the browser and operating system expose.
