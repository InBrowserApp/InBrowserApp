## Trim and Merge Audio Locally

Keep the part you need from a voice note, song, or recording. Add one file to trim it, or add several files to join their selected regions in order. Your audio stays in your browser and is never uploaded.

## Select, Arrange, and Preview

Use the waveform handles or enter start and end times in seconds. Play each selection, move clips earlier or later, and duplicate a clip to keep several sections from the same recording. Reset a trim to restore the full clip, or undo an accidental removal. Join clips directly, insert a silent pause, or overlap them with a crossfade. Each crossfade is limited to half of either adjacent clip so short clips remain usable. Create the result to preview the complete audio before downloading; you can cancel an export and keep editing.

## Formats and Audio Quality

Input support depends on your browser and the file’s codec. Common formats include MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC, and WebM. Export MP3 for sharing, WAV for uncompressed 16-bit PCM, or OGG Vorbis for open-format compressed audio. MP3 and OGG offer smaller-file, balanced, and higher-quality settings. Size estimates help you choose; OGG uses variable bitrate, so its actual size depends on the audio. Audio is decoded and exported at 48 kHz. Mono stays mono; mixed mono and stereo clips export as stereo. WAV avoids another lossy compression step but cannot restore detail lost in the source.

## Tips for Clean Cuts

Listen around the start and end of each selection. Enable smooth clip edges to apply a short 10 ms fade at exposed cuts and reduce clicks. Crossfades blend adjacent clips; silent gaps are useful between spoken sections. Large recordings use more browser memory; work with shorter clips if your device struggles.
