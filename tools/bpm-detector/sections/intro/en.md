## Find the BPM of a song or audio loop

Use the BPM Detector to estimate the tempo of a song, drum loop, or practice recording in your browser. BPM means beats per minute. A tempo of 120 BPM gives you two beats per second. The estimate can help you set a metronome, match loops to a project, or compare tracks for a DJ set.

## How to detect tempo from an audio file

1. Choose or drop a mono or stereo recording. Common inputs include WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC, and OGG, depending on your browser.
2. Check the estimated BPM. Play the original audio and listen for the pulse you would tap along to.
3. Copy the BPM into your editor, metronome, or notes. Analysis and playback run on your device.

## Why might a song have two plausible BPM readings?

You can count every pulse or group pulses into pairs. The same recording can feel like 80 BPM or 160 BPM depending on the rhythm and what you count as a beat. The half and double values let you compare those interpretations while the recording plays at its original speed.

## What makes a tempo estimate reliable?

The detector looks for recurring changes in the sound and estimates a tempo between 45 and 220 BPM. For long files it analyzes sections across the recording. Steady drums or repeated accents usually give a clearer result. A soft introduction, a performance that changes speed, or a piece with no steady pulse can make an estimate ambiguous. Try a longer excerpt from a section with a clear beat if the result is unclear.
