## Hide private details in screenshots and photos

Use Image Redactor to cover details before sharing a screenshot, photo, or document image. It is useful for hiding email addresses in a tutorial, names and phone numbers in a conversation, delivery addresses on a receipt, or faces and license plates in a photo. You can add multiple regions and give each one its own effect and settings.

## Choose solid color, blur, or pixelation

- **Solid color** covers a region with an opaque fill. Choose the color you want to use. This is the clearest choice for private text, account numbers, and other details that need to be fully covered.
- **Blur** softens the selected area. Increase the blur radius for a softer appearance, or reduce it to keep more visual context around a face or part of a photo.
- **Pixelate** turns the selected area into visible square blocks. Increase the pixel size for a coarser mosaic, or decrease it for smaller blocks.

Different regions can use different styles: for example, a solid rectangle over an email address and a blurred ellipse over a face. Solid regions stay on top where effects overlap.

## Draw and adjust regions

1. **Open an image.** Choose a file, drag it onto the tool, or paste an image from your clipboard. You can also try the built-in example first.
2. **Choose a shape and effect.** Rectangles work well for lines of text and document fields. Ellipses suit faces and rounded areas. Use Brush to paint over irregular details, adjusting the brush size as needed.
3. **Draw over the details you want to cover.** To start another region with a different effect, choose a drawing tool first, then set its style. This keeps adjustments separate from the previously selected region.
4. **Edit an existing region.** Choose Select & move and click a region. Drag it to reposition it, use the square corner handle to resize it, or drag the round top handle to rotate it. Hold Shift while rotating to snap to 15-degree steps.

Use Undo and Redo to revisit edits, delete a selected region, or clear all regions to begin again on the same image.

## Zoom, pan, and keyboard shortcuts

Zoom in to position a region over small details. When the image extends beyond the visible canvas, choose Pan and drag to move around it with a mouse or touch. Fit brings the image back into view. Panning and zooming change the view without changing the exported image dimensions.

For keyboard editing, focus the canvas and press Enter to add a centered region. Use `[` and `]` to select existing regions, then move the selection with the arrow keys; hold Shift for larger steps. The tool shortcuts are V for Select & move, H for Pan, R for Rectangle, E for Ellipse, and B for Brush. Use Ctrl/⌘ Z to undo and Shift Ctrl/⌘ Z to redo.

## Export PNG, JPEG, or WebP

- **PNG** preserves sharp text, edges, and transparency, making it a good choice for screenshots and document images.
- **JPEG** is useful for photos. Transparent areas become white, and the quality setting controls the balance between detail and file size.
- **WebP** offers compressed output with support for transparency. Adjust its quality setting to suit the image.

Downloads keep the original pixel dimensions and include the applied redactions. Copy PNG always places a PNG image on the clipboard, regardless of the selected download format.

## Local processing and supported images

Your image is processed on your device and is not uploaded. The exported file does not include the original image's metadata, such as its embedded location information.

You can open PNG, JPEG, WebP, and other image formats your browser can read. Animated images are exported as a still frame. The original file on your device is not overwritten; download the edited image as a separate file.
