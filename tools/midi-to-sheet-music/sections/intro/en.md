## Convert MIDI to sheet music and PDF

Choose **Open MIDI file** or drop a `.mid` or `.midi` file onto the tool. You can also choose **Try an example** to start with a short piano melody. The tool turns MIDI notes into sheet music and shows the result page by page. Check the rhythm in **Sheet music preview**, then choose **Download PDF** to save all the pages for printing or sharing. Conversion takes place in your browser.

## Adjust how the rhythm is written

A recorded performance can land ahead of or behind the beat. **Quantization grid** aligns note starts and ends to a rhythmic subdivision. Start with sixteenth notes (1/16). Try eighth notes (1/8) to simplify the rhythm, or thirty-second notes (1/32) to keep shorter notes distinct. The preview updates when you change the grid. Compare it with the original performance if a rest or a tied note looks unexpected.

## Choose PDF or MusicXML

Use PDF to print the score on A4 paper or read it on a tablet. Choose **Download MusicXML** to revise the notation, add fingerings, or change how the music is laid out in a score editor such as MuseScore Studio, Dorico, or Sibelius. For US Letter paper, open that file in the [MusicXML to PDF Converter](/tools/musicxml-to-pdf-converter/).

## What the score carries over

The tool reads beat-based Standard MIDI Files of format 0 or 1. It groups simultaneous notes, follows time and key signatures, and includes tempo changes. MIDI stores timing and pitch from a performance, so details such as tuplets, pickup bars, or enharmonic spelling can benefit from editing in MusicXML.
