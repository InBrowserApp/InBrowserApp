## How to test your monitor’s gradients and color levels

Start with **Grayscale**, then try **RGB gradients**, **Black levels**, and **White levels**. Choose **View fullscreen** to get a larger view of the same pattern. Keep the screen at your normal viewing angle and check the display in the room lighting you usually use.

## What color banding and tint look like

The smooth grayscale should transition gradually from black to white. Sudden stripes or edges in this ramp are signs of banding. The 16 bars below it have intentional boundaries, so check that neighboring bars remain distinct. The grayscale should also look neutral. Use the red, green, and blue ramps to check whether a particular channel shows visible breaks.

## How to check shadow and highlight detail

In **Black levels**, compare the numbered dark patches with each other and the black background. In **White levels**, check whether the pale patches remain distinguishable as they approach white. Several neighboring patches that merge into one tone can indicate crushed shadows or clipped highlights. Repeat the comparison after a small change to your display’s brightness or contrast.

## Why patterns can look different between screens

Viewing angle, reflections, browser color management, HDR settings, and night mode can all change how the patterns look. To compare two displays, use the same browser and similar settings on both. This test helps you inspect what you can see on screen. Measuring color accuracy or panel bit depth requires dedicated equipment.
